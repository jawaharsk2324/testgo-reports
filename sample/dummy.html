Treatment Timing Guidelines

Detection of BV-associated organisms does not always indicate active infection. BV treatment typically involves 5-7 days of oral or vaginal metronidazole/clindamycin per CDC guidelines. Mixed infections may require combination therapy and extended duration of 10-14 days. Resistance genes detected may necessitate extended treatment and combination therapy.

Special Considerations

Vaginal microbiome composition varies; detection of BV markers should be interpreted with symptoms like discharge and odor. Resistance genes indicate possible treatment limitations but do not guarantee resistance. Antimicrobials should be used carefully, as treatment failure may occur.

Methodology

Nucleic acids are extracted from urine samples and analyzed using Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR). The Genepath-MGN-LUTI assays detect urinary tract pathogens and antibiotic resistance markers with greater than 99% accuracy and specificity. Infection ranges—classified as high, medium, or low—were established during method validation based on corresponding DNA Copies/μL values. The clinical significance of these ranges has not been evaluated by .
